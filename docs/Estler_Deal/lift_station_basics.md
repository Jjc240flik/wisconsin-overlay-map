# LIFT STATION — PURPOSE, WHY ON OUR LAND, WHO PAYS (Estler 63.4-ac, Greenville WI)
Compiled: 2026-08-20 | Labels: CONFIRMED / LIKELY / NO RELIABLE DATA. Sources: Greenville's own Cedar Corp. Dec 2021 Wastewater Master Plan, WI project bid data, village records.

## 1. WHAT A LIFT STATION IS + WHY IT'S NEEDED — CONFIRMED (engineering fundamentals)

- **Sanitary sewer = gravity system.** Pipes slope continuously downhill so sewage flows by gravity to a treatment plant or regional main. Minimum slope keeps solids moving; too-flat or too-low terrain breaks gravity.
- **When the site is low/flat relative to the downstream main, sewage can't flow downhill** — it must be pumped UP to a point where gravity can resume.
- **A lift station (pump station) = wet well (underground storage tank) + pumps + controls (+ often odor control, standby generator).** Sewage collects in the wet well; pumps push it through a **force main** (pressurized pipe) up to a gravity main or manhole at higher elevation.
- **When required vs optional:** required whenever (a) the property is at lower elevation than the nearest gravity main, (b) distance is too far to maintain gravity slope without burying pipe impossibly deep, or (c) the topography is flat farmland with minimal fall. Optional only if a gravity connection exists at adequate depth/elevation.

## 5. ONGOING OBLIGATIONS (if the station sits on our parcel)

- **Maintenance:** typically the MUNICIPALITY takes over O&M after acceptance (standard WI practice; villages maintain their own LS systems — see Wausau/Madison/Waterford programs). Developer usually builds it, then dedicates/turns it over with a developer agreement.
- **Easement:** the village will require a permanent sanitary easement on the parcel for the station + force main route — reduces developable area (small; ~30×50 to 50×100 ft footprint + access).
- **O&M cost to us:** minimal if village takes over (they fund O&M from sewer rates). If not, $5–30K/yr.
- **Aesthetics/odor/noise:** modern stations are quiet and odor-controlled (canned pumps, carbon filters), but neighbors still object — board member Dean Culbertson raised "park and lift station concerns" re: the 4 private-road parcels. Placement on the low-ground CSM-only zone (4 big lots) minimizes impact; the master plan itself puts park+pond+lift station there.
- **Lot-value impact:** lift stations can slightly reduce adjacent lot values (stigma), but with proper buffers/odor control the impact is small. No WI case of a station killing a subdivision found (UNVERIFIED on this).

## 6. FORCE MAIN — WHAT IT IS, COST

- **Force main = pressurized sewer pipe** from the lift station up to the gravity connection point. Unlike gravity pipe (sloped, shallow), force mains can go up and over terrain.
- **Cost: $100–400/LF installed** (verified: Waterford LS15 80 LF @ $400/LF). A 1,000-ft force main = $100–400K extra. Length depends on how far the station is from the gravity connection (Everglade LS No. 1 area).
- **Implication:** the closer the station is to the gravity main / the shorter the force main, the cheaper. Doug's proposed location (low ground near the connection path) presumably minimizes force-main length vs the village's "diagonal route" — one reason the utility head supports it.
