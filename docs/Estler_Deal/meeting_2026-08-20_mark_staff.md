## KEY FINDINGS (verbatim-anchored)

### 1. LIFT STATION NEED + LOCATION (Cody/Steve/Mark)
- **"Majority of that area needs a lift station in order to send that sewer to the treatment plant... a small portion could probably get by with some gravity."**
- Master plan (5 yrs ago) had the station **in the northern portion**, but **"we don't really expect a whole lot of development up in that northern area. So potentially that lift station could be brought back to the south here. It'll kind of service all that developable area."** → **Village is OPEN to relocating the station SOUTH toward our parcel/developable area.**
- Gravity on our 4-lot area: **"a little corner might be gravity fed... everything else would kind of gravity towards the little spots and then we would pump it back up."** → Gravity is NOT a viable primary path (only a small corner).
- Station designed for: **"300 plus acres with 500 homes"** (Cody) / **"maybe 200 acres, give or take"** (Mark). Oversized for our 4-lot/44-lot development alone.
- **Other properties drain to it:** Cody — "that property, just the way you're talking, that would most likely flow to that lift station too, which would offset the cost." → Cost-sharing via assessment naturally includes other parcels.

### 2. WHO PAYS — CONFIRMED WITH PRECEDENT
- **Norm:** "Typically, Greenville requires any new development to pay for those improvements." If the station is needed just for our development → developer pays.
- **BUT area-serving stations are special-assessed:** "The stations actually cover larger areas, and **there's been assessments put on those properties and then as development comes in, they pay those assessments.**" → **The village HAS a special-assessment precedent for area stations.**
- **"The other option is for you guys to do it, and then kind of work out some sort of agreement to get paid back in the future."** (developer builds → reimbursement agreement)
- **The Board decides:** "That's something you would potentially have to ask the board... Would the village be willing to put that lift station in and then assess back the cost to all the benefiting properties?"

### 3. COST
- Steve: $2M estimate "is probably realistic" (piping + station).
- Could be tweaked for a "lesser regional, more development-focused" station.
- **$2M kills the deal if 100% developer-paid** — Jason/Adam made this explicit and staff agreed.

### 4. NO SEPTIC
- "No, because it's in the sanitary district." Municipal code requires sewer. No septic option.

### 5. TID — DEAD (direct answer)
- **"I can tell you the village is not, at this time, is not interested in doing residential TIDs. They're not."** → Kill the TID path.

### 6. LAND DONATION — CONDITIONAL
- "Eventually, **if it's the right location**."
- Station may go NORTH (on other 4-lot parcels above, possibly near Doug's area) — not necessarily on our land.
- **"I don't think we've done any engineering for any other locations... we'd have to do a feasibility."** → Station location is UNDETERMINED; our 10-14-acre donation only helps if the station lands on our parcel.
- CSM zone confirmed: max 4 lots by CSM in the western zone.

### 7. POLITICAL WARNING (off the record, Mark)
- "I wouldn't hang my hat on having the board move forward and trying to open this area up for development just because they've seen in the past that the neighborhood has come out against it."
- No turnover on PC/board since Gunderson's ordeal. Board listens to residents who speak.
- **Elected officials "take the route of the citizens that come and speak"** — Jason observed; Mark: "that's a fair statement."
- Staff (Mark/Steve/Cody) supports the area developing (comp plan + utility plans call for it). Elected = the risk.

### 8. LOT SIZE REALITY
- "We want to be around the acre... if it ends up being 0.9 acres, maybe they would allow that, but probably not much less." (A Hwy 76 developer was pushed toward 1 acre recently.)
